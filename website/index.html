Features

The parts of your phone you reach for, on your desktop

link2linux is being built feature by feature. This is what the first release is designed to do.

  • Notifications

    Phone notifications show up on your desktop. Reply inline, or dismiss them on both devices at once.

  • Messages

    Answer texts from your keyboard, straight from the notification, without picking up your phone.

  • Calls

    See who's calling and talk through your computer's speakers and microphone over Bluetooth.

  • Photos

    Send the photos you just took to your PC in a couple of taps. No cables, no cloud uploads.

  • Files

    Drag files onto the app to send them to your phone, or share them to your desktop from any app.

  • Clipboard

    Copy a link or a code on one device and paste it on the other.

  • Pairing

    Pair once by scanning a QR code. After that, your devices find each other on your network.

  • Screen Later

    Mirror your phone's screen in a window on your desktop. Planned for a release after the first.

How it works

Three steps to connect

No account to create and nothing to configure on your router.

  1. Install both apps

    Get link2linux for your Linux desktop and its companion app for your Android phone.

  2. Scan the QR code

    Your desktop shows a code and your phone scans it. The two devices exchange their own keys, so only they can talk to each other.

  3. Turn on what you want

    Every feature starts off. Switch on the ones you need, and Android asks for each permission only at that moment.

Privacy & security

Your data stays between your devices

Your phone holds your messages, photos and codes, so link2linux is designed to keep them on hardware you own.

  • Stays on your network

    Your phone and computer connect directly over your local network. There's no account, no cloud relay and no server in between.

  • Encrypted, with keys you paired

    Every connection uses TLS 1.3. Each device creates its own keys and only accepts the device it was paired with.

  • You choose what's shared

    Each feature stays off until you turn it on, and you can switch it off again at any time.

  • Open source

    All the code is public under the Apache-2.0 license, so anyone can read it, audit it and build it.

Download

Install on your computer and your phone

The desktop app runs on Linux with GNOME, KDE Plasma and other desktops, on Wayland or X11. The first releases ship it as a .deb, an .rpm and an AppImage for 64-bit x86 PCs; Flatpak and ARM64 builds follow. The phone app needs Android 8.0 or newer.

Pre-release v0.1.2.

Linux desktop

  • One-line install

    Picks the .deb, .rpm or AppImage for your system, checks it against SHA256SUMS and installs it.

    Read the script
    curl -fsSL https://codesensei-tushar.github.io/link2linux/install.sh | sh
  • .deb package

    Ubuntu, Debian, Linux Mint, Pop!_OS.

    Download .deb
    sudo apt install ./link2linux_amd64.deb
  • .rpm package

    Fedora, openSUSE and other RPM-based systems.

    Download .rpm
    sudo dnf install ./link2linux.x86_64.rpm
  • AppImage

    Any distribution, without installing. Needs FUSE 2.

    Download AppImage
    chmod +x link2linux-x86_64.AppImage
./link2linux-x86_64.AppImage
  • Flatpak Later

    From Flathub, sandboxed, with automatic updates.

Android phone

  • APK

    Android 8.0 and newer.

    Download APK
    1. Open the downloaded file on your phone.
    2. If Android asks, allow your browser to install apps.
    3. Open link2linux and scan the code your desktop shows.
  • App stores Later

    Store listings follow the first releases, with updates handled by the store.

Verify a download

Every release includes a SHA256SUMS file. Put it next to your download and run:

sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS --ignore-missing
FAQ

Questions, answered

When can I download it?

Now. Version 0.1.2 is out as a pre-release: it pairs your phone and shows its notifications on your desktop. The Download section has every package. Messages, calls, photos, files and the clipboard follow in later releases; watch the repository on GitHub to hear when they land.

Is it free?

Yes. link2linux is free and open source under the Apache-2.0 license.

Does it need an internet connection?

No. Your phone and computer only need to be on the same local network, and they connect to each other directly. Call audio uses Bluetooth.

Does it work with an iPhone?

No. link2linux is built for Android phones.

How can I help?

Star and watch the repository, open an issue with ideas or problems, or contribute code and documentation on GitHub.
